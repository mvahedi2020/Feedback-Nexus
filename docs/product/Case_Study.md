# Feedback Nexus — decision brief

## User and problem

A B2B SaaS PM preparing a planning discussion needs to compare customer feedback without losing the original words or account context. In the fictional Northstar workspace, import recovery and saved reporting compete for attention. The product question is whether a reviewer can inspect the evidence behind a planning recommendation rather than inherit an unexplained rank.

## Product choice

The prototype is an evidence board, not an automatic roadmap. It preserves individual signals, links related feedback, shows unique affected accounts, exposes impact, confidence, and effort, and lets the PM record a rationale and planning state. The visible comparison is `affected accounts × impact × confidence ÷ effort`; it starts a conversation and does not make a decision.

F01 and F02 demonstrate the choice. Their original quotes remain separate while the group shows the four-account union: Alder Labs, Finch Studio, Cedar Systems, and Pineworks. F04 remains a distinct saved-report alternative. Simulated tags are editable support, not evaluated assistance, and their review is optional.

## Cost and boundary

Traceability costs time: the PM must inspect evidence and explain a choice. Unique account reach also omits contract value and strategic importance. Browser-local state, export, Undo, and reset make a safe portfolio walkthrough possible, but do not provide shared history, permissions, or operational durability. The fictional sample does not connect to customers, a CRM, or a model.

## Evidence and next investment

Software checks show that the documented workflow and recovery paths ran; they do not show demand, usability, or outcomes. No customer interviews or human study have occurred. The next investment is a five-participant comparison with each person’s current method, using consented fictional data and the published scoring protocol. Expand scope only if participants can trace the source evidence, explain the score and simulated-tag boundary, and make a justified decision without critical misunderstanding.
