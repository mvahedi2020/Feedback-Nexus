# Feedback Nexus — an explainable prioritization decision

## Problem hypothesis

A PM can lose context when comments become tickets. Keeping original words, affected accounts, confidence and rationale together may improve a planning conversation. This has not been validated with customers.

## User and scenario

A B2B SaaS PM prepares a weekly planning discussion. At fictional Northstar, import recovery, saved reporting and administration compete for attention. All quotes and accounts are illustrative.

## My product role

Mo Vahedi's personal case study covers problem definition, priorities, requirements, workflow design and evaluation. Google Antigravity and other AI tools assisted with implementation and verification. This does not represent a staffed product team.

## Alternatives and decisions

An inbox alone collects noise. An automatic roadmap hides judgment. The selected scope preserves quotes, groups related signals, exposes assumptions and records a decision.

Score = affected accounts × impact × confidence ÷ effort. Account count avoids fictional revenue estimates, but differs from strategic importance. Grouping preserves originals and shows unique accounts; each signal retains its own score. Do not add scores across duplicates.

The AI angle is accountable review: suggestions are simulated and editable. No accuracy claim is made. Live CRM ingestion, model training, customer messaging and delivery dates are excluded. Browser storage makes evaluation easy but does not support collaboration.

## Evidence and next validation

Passing software checks establishes interaction behavior, not demand or usability. Recruit five PMs who triage weekly, give each an eight-minute prioritization task, ask them to explain their selection, and compare with their normal workflow. Use consented, sanitized examples. No sessions have been conducted.

## What the product question made me trade off

The question was not “can a system rank feedback automatically?” It was whether a PM can make a planning recommendation that another person can interrogate. That led to a deliberately smaller Northstar task: compare F01/F02 import recovery with a saved-report signal, preserve the customer words, adjust a visible directional score, and state why one item advances. I chose an evidence board over a polished automatic roadmap because automation could make the answer look more certain than the fictional inputs deserve.

That choice has limits. Unique-account reach does not capture contract value or strategic importance. The simulated tag is editable but has no accuracy evaluation. Browser-local storage supports a safe demonstration but offers no shared history, access control, or operational durability. Technical checks show the sample paths ran; the current material includes no customer interviews, purchase intent, adoption, retention, or business outcome.

The next investment is therefore the Discovery Plan, not integrations or more automation. The proposed five sessions and scoring rubric would reveal whether PMs can trace evidence, explain the score, distinguish a suggestion from a decision, and recover from a change.
