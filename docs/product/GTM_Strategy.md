# Commercial hypotheses

Feedback Nexus has two hypothetical audiences with different reasons to care. The **user** is a B2B SaaS PM or product-operations lead who must turn scattered feedback into a planning recommendation. The **buyer**, if a real product earned investment, may be a product leader responsible for consistent prioritization across a team. The user needs preserved quotes, accounts, and an explainable choice; the buyer would need confidence that the workflow produces a reviewable record without adding unmanageable process. Neither audience has been interviewed for this sample.

Test the buyer and user separately before proposing packaging. Give five target users the F01/F02 and F04 decision against their current method, then ask what evidence they needed and whether they would repeat the workflow. In a separate buyer conversation, show the resulting rationale record and ask what governance or review problem it would need to solve before a team could sponsor it. Do not ask for price or imply an offer until repeated problem evidence establishes a use case.

A hypothetical first adoption test is an opt-in, consented exercise with five target users using the fictional Northstar import-recovery task. Activation would mean completing the evidence-to-decision workflow and accurately explaining the score and simulated-tag boundary. Stop investment in packaging, collaboration, or integrations if two or more participants cannot trace source evidence, misread the score as automatic, or prefer their current method without a specific remediable gap. Four of five unassisted completions is a learning threshold, not proof of retention or demand. No outreach, waitlist, partner, marketplace listing, conversion, adoption, or revenue result is claimed.

Further investment needs repeated problem evidence, a comparison against current spreadsheet or discovery-tool workflows, and a reason a purchaser would fund shared controls. Privacy, access, retention, collaboration, integrations, and any live assistance require their own validated requirements first.
